# SLAY LinkedIn Posts

Write LinkedIn posts the way Lara Acosta does — the creator who grew from 0 to 250K+ followers in under two years. Her insight: even basic advice feels original when wrapped in a personal story nobody else can tell. The story is the moat. Everything else in this skill exists to support that.

## Step 0: Load Kyle's voice

Before drafting anything, read `references/voice-tone-guide.md`. It's Kyle's personal voice and tone spec — direct & confident, casual-professional, story-first, real stories only, plus a "never do" list (no corporate jargon, no hedge openers, no hashtag walls, no rage-bait). Its LinkedIn playbook is this skill's SLAY structure with Kyle's voice already layered on, so Steps 1-4 below already comply — use the guide to resolve any word-choice or tone judgment call, not to reinvent the structure. For X/Twitter posts, use Part 3 of the guide (hook → hard line → conviction) instead of the LinkedIn steps below.

## Step 1: Get a real story first

Before writing anything, interview the user. Never invent a story for them — a fabricated story defeats the entire framework and readers can smell it. Ask 2-3 of these (pick the most relevant, don't interrogate):

- What's the moment this lesson actually hit you? (a specific day, conversation, failure, decision)
- What did the "before" look like? Numbers, feelings, low points — the messier the better.
- What's the contrast? (then vs. now, expectation vs. reality, what everyone says vs. what you found)
- Who is the one person you're writing this for?

If the user already gave a story in their prompt, work with it — but ask one follow-up for a concrete detail (a number, a date, a quote someone said) if the story is vague. Specifics are what make stories credible: "I lasted 6 months in my sales job" beats "I had a bad job."

If the user truly has no story and says so, fall back to a "borrowed observation" opening (something they witnessed or a pattern they noticed) — but flag that personal stories outperform.

## Step 2: Structure the post

Use this skeleton. Read `references/structure.md` for the full anatomy with examples before writing your first post in a conversation.

1. **Hook** (line 1) — grabs attention. Transformation, tension, or contrarian claim.
2. **Re-hook** (line 2) — adds curiosity, often in parentheses. This is Lara's actual viral lever, not the hook. "(And it's not what you think)" / "(Without posting daily)"
3. **Lead** — one line telling the reader exactly what they're about to learn.
4. **Story** — the user's real story, compressed. Touch the struggle briefly, cut all fluff.
5. **Lesson** — the single insight the story earned. One idea per post (Rule of 1).
6. **Actionable advice** — concrete steps the reader can do today. Lists with arrows (→) or dashes.
7. **You** — end by turning it to the reader: a question, a challenge, or "Your turn."

## Step 3: Apply the formatting rules

LinkedIn truncates after ~2-3 lines ("...see more"), so the hook + re-hook must work standing alone. Format for skimming:

- One sentence per line. Blank line between most lines.
- No paragraph longer than 2 lines.
- Lists use `-` or `→` (the ↳ arrow for sub-points/examples).
- No jargon, no corporate speak, no hashtag walls (0-3 hashtags max, or none).
- 150-300 words is the sweet spot. Under 100 for pure-story posts is fine.
- Write at a 5th-grade reading level. Short words win.

## Step 4: Pressure-test before delivering

Check the draft against these — fix anything that fails:

- **Rule of 1**: one niche, one idea, one reader. If the post teaches two things, cut one.
- **Truncation test**: do the first two lines alone make someone click "see more"?
- **Specificity test**: at least 2 concrete details (numbers, timeframes, names of feelings). "Grew my following" fails; "0 to 17,389 followers in 12 months" passes.
- **So-what test**: could the reader act on this in the next 24 hours?
- **Voice test**: read it aloud mentally. If a sentence wouldn't be said to a friend, rewrite it.

## Output format

In chat: deliver the post in a code block (so it copies clean with line breaks intact), then below it, 2-3 alternate hook + re-hook pairs the user can swap in. Briefly note which SLAY element each section maps to only if the user asks.

If saving to a file: the file must contain ONLY the final post text, exactly as it would be pasted into LinkedIn — no markdown headers, no code fences, no alternates, no notes. Put alternates and commentary in your chat response instead. The user will copy or upload this file directly, so anything extra would end up in their post.
