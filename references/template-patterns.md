# Post pattern library

30 post archetypes derived from Lara Acosta's "30 Days of Templates" (her free lead magnet — patterns described here in original wording; see her Notion for the originals). Use this when the user needs a post angle, wants variety across a content week, or asks "what should I post today?"

Each pattern still goes through the full SLAY treatment from SKILL.md: real specifics from the user, hook + re-hook opening, Rule of 1, pressure-test before delivering.

## How to pick

- Match the pattern to what the user HAS: a result → use a transformation/receipt pattern; an opinion → use a contrarian pattern; expertise but no story → use a teaching pattern.
- Across a week, vary categories — don't run three contrarian posts back to back.

## Category 1: Transformation & proof (storytell pillar)

1. **Then vs. now** — two dated snapshots ("December 2021: X. December 2022: Y."), then the actions that bridged them. Needs real dates and numbers.
2. **I quit / I stopped** — what the user stopped doing, the misconceptions people assume about quitting it, and the results since. Ends with why they'd never go back.
3. **How it happened** — one specific recent action → the results it triggered → the reasons it worked → "and I didn't even need to do [the hard thing everyone assumes]."
4. **Job escape** — short stint in a bad fit ("I lasted X months in..."), what was wrong, the leap, the constraints they had anyway, and the today-list.
5. **Experience earned** — "X years ago I had zero [niche] experience. Today [achievement]." Then the learning loop that got them there.
6. **Years of growth ladder** — year-by-year worse positions climbing to now. Each rung one line.
7. **Failure résumé** — counter the highlight reel: list real failures this year and what they reveal. "Not enough people post 'I struggled with X.'"
8. **Hidden backstory** — "99% of my followers don't know this" + the failed ventures before the current thing. Lesson: stop quitting so soon.
9. **Behind the success** — what people see (the win) vs. what they should see (the messy story behind it). Anti-comparison post.

## Category 2: Contrarian & myth-busting (educate pillar)

10. **Think vs. do** — how people think you succeed at X (wrong list) vs. how you actually do (right list).
11. **Harsh truth** — "[Doing the obvious thing] is only half the job." Then the neglected other half as a you-need-to list. Stop/start close.
12. **Please stop** — one common practice to kill, why it fails, and the replacement steps.
13. **Expectation vs. reality of the craft** — what outsiders think the job is vs. what it actually requires.
14. **Permission with a catch** — "You can do A, B, C... but if you don't do D, none of it works." Knowing-vs-doing close.
15. **Simple, not easy** — the intimidating version of the task (complicated list) vs. the simplified version that actually works.
16. **Simplify** — "People overcomplicate [thing]." Avoid-list vs. instead-list, scoped to beginners with a timeframe.

## Category 3: Teaching & value (educate pillar)

17. **Straight how-to** — numbered actions, the anti-pattern to avoid, and a timeframe challenge.
18. **The hack** — one specific trick, why it works (named like a principle: "the X Effect"), outcomes it unlocks.
19. **Secret hack (steal this)** — same as above but framed as insider knowledge being given away.
20. **One lesson** — a single piece of earned advice, expanded with arrowed explanations. Give-don't-take framing.
21. **Tips with a focus shift** — quick tip, the actions that follow from it, then "↳ focus on [the thing people miss]."
22. **Stop/start lists** — "You don't need X. You need Y." Mirror-image stop and start lists.
23. **Honest advice** — "My honest advice for everyone on [platform]:" backed by the achievement that earned the right to give it.
24. **Value-first positioning** — "No one cares what you do. They care what you can do for them." Then the advice list.
25. **Book that changed everything** — one book (not a cliché pick), what it taught, "taught me more than my degree."

## Category 4: Strategy & self-disclosure (mixed pillars)

26. **My strategy, broken down** — current working strategy as a short list, then "too simple? let's break it down" with the weekly actions. Explain why it works for YOU specifically.
27. **What I needed vs. didn't** — passive states that didn't move the needle vs. actions that did. Off-season/athlete framing works here.
28. **Lifetime goals** — the goals set long ago, when they were hit, and what carried the user there.
29. **Worried about X?** — name a fear holding readers back (posting, selling, visibility), diagnose why it happens, give the workaround steps.
30. **Comparison trap** — call out the platform's highlight-reel effect directly; pair every visible success with its invisible cost.

## Adaptation rules

- Templates are skeletons, not scripts. Fill them ONLY with the user's real material — a template plus invented facts is worse than no template.
- The template's natural ending is often weak; replace with a proper "You" close per structure.md.
- If two patterns fit, pick the one whose proof the user can make most specific.
