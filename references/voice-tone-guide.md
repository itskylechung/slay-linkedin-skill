# Kyle's Voice & Tone Guide

One voice, two playbooks. The core identity never changes between platforms — only format, length, and edge do.

---

## Part 1: Core voice (shared, non-negotiable)

### Identity

A builder sharing the founder journey in public. Writes for founders, builders, and ambitious professionals. The reader should feel like they're getting real notes from someone one step ahead — not a guru broadcasting from a stage.

### Voice in one line

**Direct and confident, casual-professional, story-first.** Says what he thinks, backs it with what he's actually done, and keeps it human.

### Tone attributes

| Attribute | In practice |
|---|---|
| Direct & confident | Take a clear stance in line one. No "I think maybe" or "just my 2 cents." |
| Casual-professional | Conversational but polished. Contractions yes, slang sparingly, emojis rarely (0–1 per post, never in the hook). |
| Selectively open | Share lessons from failures — numbers and specifics welcome — but not every doubt or private detail. Vulnerability serves the lesson, not the drama. |
| Real stories only | Never fabricate or exaggerate. Specifics are the moat: "$0 → $4k MRR in 5 months" beats "grew revenue." |

### Topics

- Building in public: what I'm working on, decisions, trade-offs
- Wins **and** failures — always with the lesson extracted
- Founder lessons: hiring, shipping, focus, money, mindset

### Never do (any platform)

- **Corporate jargon**: no "leverage," "synergy," "circle back," "value-add," "thought leadership"
- Hedge-everything openers ("In my humble opinion…")
- Hashtag walls, engagement bait ("Agree?"), forced polls
- Preaching about things I haven't done
- Rage-bait — only post takes I'd defend in the replies

### Universal quality bar

- At least 2 concrete details per post: numbers, timeframes, real quotes
- One idea, one reader, one post (Rule of 1)
- Voice test: read aloud — if I wouldn't say it to a friend over coffee, rewrite it

## Part 2: LinkedIn playbook (SLAY framework)

**Register:** Warmer end of my voice. Room for a narrative arc. Confident but fair — clear stance, one line of acknowledged nuance when it matters.

**Structure (every post):**

1. **Hook** (line 1) — transformation, tension, or contrarian claim
2. **Re-hook** (line 2) — curiosity in parentheses: "(And it's not what you think)"
3. **Lead** — one line: what the reader is about to learn
4. **Story** — my real story, compressed; touch the struggle, cut the fluff
5. **Lesson** — the single insight the story earned
6. **Actionable advice** — steps with `→` or `-`
7. **You** — turn to the reader: question, challenge, or "Your turn."

**Format rules:**

- One sentence per line, blank line between most lines
- No paragraph over 2 lines; hook + re-hook must survive the "…see more" truncation
- 150–300 words (under 100 fine for pure-story posts)
- 0–3 hashtags or none; 5th-grade reading level

**Pre-post test:** Do the first two lines alone make someone click "see more"? Can the reader act on it within 24 hours?

## Part 3: X playbook (hook → hard line → conviction)

**Register:** Sharpest end of my voice. Shorter, punchier, more edge. X rewards taking one side — the nuance lives in the replies, not the post. Still only takes I actually hold and will defend.

**Structure:**

1. **Hook line** — stops an eighth-of-a-second scroll. Address a specific reader ("My advice to every first-time founder:") or drop a credibility/number bomb
2. **Hard line** — take one side for the rest of the post. Cut hedging words: "maybe," "sometimes," "for some people," "it depends"
3. **Supporting copy** — 1–3 punchy lines; absolute claims and hyper-specific details ("20 hours a week casting spells," not "playing video games")
4. **Optional double-down reply** — lean in harder, never soften

**Format rules:**

- As short as possible — cut every word that doesn't earn its place
- No hashtags, no emojis, no "🧵 thread" labels
- Numbers in the hook when available
- Write for one target reader; act like the take applies to everyone

**Cross-posting:** Write the LinkedIn version first, then compress to X: keep the hook and hardest line, drop the story arc, sharpen the stance.

**Pre-post test:** Would line 1 stop my target reader mid-scroll? Does it teach, entertain, or strike emotion? Would I defend it in the replies tomorrow?
