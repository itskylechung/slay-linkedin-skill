# Lara Acosta's LinkedIn growth strategy

Read this when the user asks about growing on LinkedIn beyond a single post — cadence, engagement tactics, funnels, or "how do I grow like Lara." Individual post writing stays in SKILL.md and structure.md.

## The 4-3-2-1 content engine

- **4 posts a week** — non-negotiable, but NOT daily. Quality and consistency beat volume.
- **3 content pillars** — educate (teach your niche), storytell (personal narrative), sell (offers, proof).
- **2 audiences** — followers (broad growth posts) and customers (niche conversion posts). Alternate; don't write every post for everyone.
- **1 lead magnet per week** — convert reach into email. LinkedIn is rented land; email is owned.

When planning a user's week, slot their posts into pillars and audiences explicitly. A common failure: all four posts educate the same broad audience and nothing converts.

## Engagement before content (cold start)

For accounts under ~1K followers or just starting: the first 3 weeks should be mostly commenting, not posting.

- Find 10-20 creators in the user's industry.
- Leave valuable, specific comments daily — never "Great post!". A good comment adds an example, a counterpoint, or a one-line story.
- Why it works: a sharp comment on a 100K-follower account gets seen by more relevant people than a post to 200 followers.

## Authority engagement (borrowed audiences)

Keep a short daily list of bigger accounts ("the inner circle"). Engage meaningfully, help them with your expertise, build the relationship before you need it. Reciprocity from one big account outperforms months of solo posting.

## Optimize for comments, not likes

Comments drive LinkedIn distribution. Design for them:

- End every post with a question or challenge (see structure.md "Endings that get comments").
- Reply to every comment in the first hour — replies count as comments and extend reach.
- Write posts people can disagree with productively. Mild contrarianism starts conversations; pure consensus dies quietly.

## Proof stacking

Rotate visible proof into the content mix: client results, testimonials, media appearances, the user's own milestone numbers. The user's growth story is itself content — milestone posts ("0 to X in Y months") reliably outperform.

## Funnel mechanics

- Lead magnet CTAs must be compelling and specific: "Grab my free 7-day template" beats "Link in comments."
- LinkedIn suppresses links nobody clicks — weak CTA quality hurts the post's reach, not just conversions.
- Funnel: post → lead magnet → email list → paid offer. Every week should feed it once.

## The commitment floor

Minimum 3 months of weekly posting before judging results. Growth is flat-then-sudden; most people quit during the flat part. Consistency alone beats 99% of users because almost nobody sustains it.

## Reference timeline (for calibrating user expectations)

Lara's own numbers: 0 → 17K followers in year 1 → 156K in 2 years → 250K+, built into a multi-six-figure business from one platform and one funnel. Tell users early months with low numbers are the norm, not a failure signal.
