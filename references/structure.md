# Post anatomy with examples

## Why re-hooks beat hooks

Lara's words: "It's not writing better hooks." Most writers spend everything on line 1. But line 1 only buys you line 2. The re-hook is what converts a scroll-pause into a "see more" click, because it opens a curiosity gap the reader must close.

Hook patterns (line 1):
- Transformation: "I packed a one-way flight, and it changed my life."
- Before/after with numbers: "December 2021: failed dropshipping business. December 2022: 17,389 followers."
- Contrarian: "Posting 3x a week isn't enough to grow on here."
- Confession: "I lasted 6 months in my sales job."
- Authority claim: "How I grew to 80K followers in 15 months."

Re-hook patterns (line 2):
- Negation of assumption: "(And it's not what you think)"
- Removal of expected cost: "(Without posting daily)"
- Stakes raise: "Neither are selfies."
- Insider promise: "(From someone who just figured it out)"

## Full example, annotated

```
I grew to 80K LinkedIn followers in 15 months.        ← Hook (authority + numbers)

(Without posting daily)                                ← Re-hook (removes expected cost)

Here's the rule that made it possible:                 ← Lead

When I started, I did what everyone said.              ← Story begins
Posted every day. Burned out in 3 weeks.
Got 11 likes, mostly from my mum.

Then I deleted my content calendar.                    ← Struggle → turn
I picked ONE niche. ONE format. ONE reader.

That's the Rule of 1.                                  ← Lesson (single idea)

How to apply it this week:                             ← Actionable advice
→ Write down the 1 person your content serves
→ Pick the 1 topic you could talk about for a year
→ Master 1 post format before touching another

Consistency in one direction beats activity in ten.

What's your ONE topic?                                 ← You (turn to reader)
```

## Story compression

A story in a LinkedIn post is 3-6 lines, not a memoir. The formula:
1. Where I was (1-2 lines, with a concrete low point)
2. The turn (1 line — the decision or realization)
3. Where it led (1-2 lines, with a concrete result)

Cut: scene-setting, weather, what anyone was wearing, every adjective you can live without. Keep: numbers, dates, direct quotes, named feelings.

## Endings that get comments

The "Y" matters because comments drive LinkedIn reach more than likes. End with:
- A binary question ("Agree or disagree?") — easy to answer, high volume
- A specific prompt ("What's your ONE topic?") — better-quality comments
- A challenge ("Try it for 3 weeks. Tell me what happens.")
Avoid: "Thoughts?" (too generic), multiple questions (splits the response).

## P.S. lines (optional)

Lara often adds a P.S. for a soft CTA — a lead magnet, an event, or a human aside. One line only. Never let the P.S. introduce a second idea that competes with the post.
